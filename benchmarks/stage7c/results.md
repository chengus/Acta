## What these columns support

The size and count columns are exact and reproducible. Statistics cost this
schema 160 bytes for `minmax` and 96 for `automatic` across four blocks, on a
67,864-byte file: 0.24% and 0.14%. `automatic` omits the four primary-column
pairs, retaining the eight non-primary ones. Rerunning reproduces these numbers
exactly, and the harness fails if two repetitions of one policy disagree.

The timing columns are best-of-five wall clock on one host and support only one
comparison: writing statistics costs ingestion throughput, and not writing them
costs none. Across repeated runs `none` is consistently the fastest, while
`minmax` and `automatic` trade places within a spread comparable to their
distance from each other. Do not read a difference between the two enabled
policies out of this table, and do not treat any of these figures as a
threshold; regenerate them on the host in question instead.

## What these columns deliberately do not show

`pruned` is 3 for every policy, which is the point rather than a defect. Stage 5
prunes on the mandatory primary bounds in the block header, and no reader path
consumes optional column statistics, so the range scan must return the same
answer whether they are present or not. That makes this table a measurement of
cost with pruning held fixed, not a cost-versus-benefit trade.

The `Automatic` thresholds are therefore not derived from a measured pruning
benefit; none exists to measure yet. They are a conservative size floor, stated
exactly on `WriterStatistics::Automatic`. Revisit them against measured pruning
when Stage 9 adds statistics-based pruning to the reader.
